WHY THIS REALM CONSUMES IT: a book that leaves this studio may carry a rights
page, and a rights page is a licence. The house already has one licence
grammar and it is DATA - three grants in a fixed order, revocability fixed by
law rather than by declaration, `exclusive` typed as the literal `false` so
the compiler itself refuses the other value, and the 90/10 as `HOUSE_SPLIT`,
exported to be OPTED INTO rather than assumed. Writing a second one in a
Svelte room would have been a second copy of a truth this app does not own.

`permits` HOLDS OPEN STRINGS, and the drawer offers free words for that
reason: the v1 vocabulary is a stroke KP ⚛ reserved, and this room does not
invent a closed list to fill the gap.

THE LAWYER GATE IS NOT THIS REPO'S TO CLOSE. `LAWYER_GATE` rides inside
`render().text`, on `render().gate`, in `draw().flagged` and in
`evidence().told`; there is no parameter anywhere on the water's surface that
suppresses it, and nothing in this app may soften it. The bind room shows
`render(it).text` WHOLE, gate included, and hands `render(it).gate` to
the-binder as `front.rights.notice`, so the warning is printed into the book
as well as onto the screen. A rendered licence is a DRAFT until a real
lawyer has read it.

HOW THE-BINDER RECEIVES IT - AS KEYS, NEVER AS AN IMPORT. The-binder declares
its own `Rights` / `RightsGrant` shapes keyed to this water's
(`holder` · `grants[].name` · `permits` · `revocable` · `exclusive` · `split`)
and reads them as keys, interpreting no term. So `src/lib/bind.ts` DRAWS with
this water and then hands the-binder a plain object of those keys. Two waters
stand side by side in this repo and neither imports the other - the Law of the
Spring, kept on both sides of the room.
