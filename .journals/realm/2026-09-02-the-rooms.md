## THE CHOICES, and why

1. **A plain rail, not the-cumdach.** The mother derives her Sidebar's panels
   from `derive`/`rederive` because she has nine rooms and a fold to arrange.
   Five doors always fit, so that arithmetic would be answering a question
   nobody asked. What is kept is the **idiom** — the family's tokens, the
   wordmark, one `QUANTUM_COLORS` face per door — written fresh, not copied.
   Every door is a real `<a href>`, so tab, Enter and the back button work
   without a line of code, and the current one carries `aria-current="page"`.
   **The ComfortBar did not come back**, per the plan.

2. **THE ONE SHAPE THAT MOVED: the six nouns are now type aliases.**
   The-panti's `sortData`/`filterData` are written
   `<T extends Record<string, unknown>>`. TypeScript gives an object **type
   alias** an implicit index signature and an **interface** none — measured
   both ways before anything was changed — so `Part[]` declared as an interface
   is *refused* by `sortData` and the identical shape declared as an alias is
   accepted. The three lawful answers were: edit the mirror (forbidden), cast
   at every call site (a lie repeated forty times), or move the shape. The
   shape moved: `Work · Part · Era · Character · Arc · Appearance` are
   `export type X = { … }` and the reason is written into `types.ts` above
   them. It costs nothing here — nothing declaration-merges and nothing
   `implements` — but it is a real change to S1's file and it is named rather
   than slipped in.

3. **The autosave writes through `updatePart` and nothing else.** 800 ms of
   quiet, and immediately on blur, on switching parts, on leaving the room and
   on `beforeunload`. The draft is captured **before** the await, so a hand
   that keeps typing during a write leaves the state at `unsaved` and schedules
   another — no keystroke is ever reported saved that was not. The three words
   on screen are `unsaved · saving · saved`, in those words.

4. **The-binder's law, carried to the byte.** The textarea's value goes to
   `updatePart` untouched: no trim, no normalisation, no reflow, no smart
   quotes, no collapsing of runs, no stripped trailing newline. The **title**
   is left alone the same way, because it is the author's text too. *"Typos are
   fingerprints unless he says otherwise."*

5. **The word count is data.** `words` is read off the row `updatePart` hands
   back, and the store replaces that one row with it rather than re-reading the
   list. Nothing in the window ever counts a word. While there are unsaved
   keystrokes the desk says **"as of the last save"** instead of guessing.

6. **`src/lib/board.ts` imports types and nothing else** — which is what lets
   the proof run **the real file** in node. Every import in it is `import type`
   and Node 24 strips them, so nothing has to resolve at runtime. `npx tsx`
   was *not* used: it is not in `node_modules` and installing it is forbidden.

7. **A move is ids and nothing else, and that is structural, not a promise.**
   `reorderWithin` returns `string[]`. There is nowhere in that file to put a
   body, a title or a timestamp, so `reorderParts(workId, ids)` is the only
   call a drag can lead to. The proof reads `base.ts`'s own text and confirms
   the payload is `{ workId, ids }`.

8. **Every drag has a keyboard twin, and the twin is the same function.**
   Cards: **Earlier / Later** buttons (`nudge`) and an era `<select>`.
   Columns: **◀ / ▶** (`nudge` over the era ids) and inline rename. The mouse
   and the keyboard call `orderParts` / `orderEras` / `place` — one road.

9. **A card's column comes from an appearance row, and a part in two eras
   shows in both.** Data is truth. Consequently a drop *inside a column the
   card is already in* is a reorder only — it does not re-place, because
   re-placing would collapse a two-era part to one behind the author's back.
   A drop into a **different** column does move it, and the era `<select>`
   sets one era, as the plan words it. Proven both ways.

10. **The reorder always sends the WHOLE chapter list.** `reorder_parts` writes
    `ord = position` for every id it is given; a partial list would leave two
    chapters wearing the same ordinal. So a drag computes
    `reorderWithin(allChapterIds, moved, targetIndex)` and sends all of it.

11. **Threads are drawn from measurement, not from a layout model.** One
    polyline per arc through the centres of the cards it appears in, in board
    order (column by column, card by card); a part with two cards is visited
    twice, because that is the board on the screen. Colours are four distinct
    `QUANTUM_COLORS` chosen by `shapeRank` — the *rule* lives in `board.ts`,
    the *colours* in the room, because that file imports types only.
    Recomputed by a `$effect` over the cards, the arcs and the appearance rows
    (so: after every reorder and every refresh) and by a `ResizeObserver` on
    the plane plus a window `resize`. The observer re-attaches when the plane
    appears, so a board that began empty is watched the moment it stops being.
    **Nothing about a thread is animated** — no transition, no draw-on — so
    `prefers-reduced-motion` is honoured by construction; the one thing that
    fades is a lifted card's opacity, and the media query removes even that
    (as `app.css` does app-wide besides).

12. **A one-card thread is counted in the legend and not drawn.** A line needs
    two ends. The legend says how many cards each arc runs through, so a real
    arc is never invisible without explanation.

13. **The cast derives and keeps nothing.** A character's appearances are the
    same `appearances` rows the board marks from, joined in the window to the
    parts they name and — through those parts' own placement rows — to the
    eras. There is no second list and no second store, which is the sixth
    noun's whole point.

14. **The-panti for every list, and greppable.** The shelf sorts works by
    `updated_at` desc; the desk sorts chapters and scenes by `ord` asc and
    narrows titles with `filterData` (contains); the board sorts eras by `ord`,
    cast and arcs by name; the cast sorts by name and narrows by name. The
    proof asserts that **all four rooms that show a list import `$lib/panti`**
    and prints their paths.

  quiet window is the one case where the `beforeunload` handler is the only

  of every gesture exists partly for this reason.
- **The threads' geometry is proven as arithmetic, not as pixels.**
  `centerOf` and `polylinePoints` are proven; whether an overlay `<svg>` with

  a thing only an opened window can say.
